This is a product built to make reading in English easier.

If any of these feelings sound familiar:

1. You do not want to miss the high-quality content available on the English internet, and you do not want your information sources limited to the Chinese internet.
2. You want to read English content in its original form, but looking up one word per sentence completely breaks the flow, or even makes the text unreadable.
3. You want to improve your English by reading articles you actually care about, rather than studying in a dull way or relying too heavily on translation tools.

Then Read Easy may be worth trying.

Read Easy focuses on English reading and combines it with AI to provide a set of genuinely useful features.

It started as a browser extension and later expanded into a web version.

During development, I posted on Jike to show what a rendered article looked like and get a better sense of what people actually needed.

## Browser Extension

Original launch post on Jike:
<a href="https://m.okjike.com/originalPosts/682452f6ca977f7fc75157c0?s=ewoidSI6ICI1YTE4ZmM1YTlkYjAzZDAwMTZlZjZlNjQiCn0=" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer">View original post</a>

When you do not need it, Read Easy stays out of the way and leaves no annoying visual residue behind.

But when you really want to sit down and read an English article carefully, one click takes you into Read Easy's reading mode.

In reading mode, Read Easy offers several different reading styles to fit different situations.

Ordered by reading difficulty:
Original > Marked > Mixed > Bilingual

Among them, **Marked** is the core feature of Read Easy. It keeps the original English text, but uses AI to add annotations such as:

automatic translation of difficult, unfamiliar, or important words; automatic marking of names, places, and brands; automatic sentence splitting for long and difficult sentences; and paragraph summaries.

Once these annotations are added, reading English starts to feel much less intimidating.

Reading may be enjoyable for some people, but for many others it is not easy, especially when the text is long and intellectually dense. That is true even in Chinese.

Read Easy's **Marked** mode mainly lowers the language barrier. To actually finish and understand an article, you still need to put in some effort. It is not as easygoing as Duolingo, but I believe what it gives back can also be much more substantial.

Recently I used Read Easy to read long essays on Wait But Why. The author's writing is extremely interesting, but also extremely long. With Read Easy's help, I managed to finish both the huge AI series and the Fermi Paradox essay. It took some effort, but it was worth it.

As for the other reading modes, Original, Mixed, and Bilingual, similar ideas already exist in some products on the market. In Read Easy they mainly serve as support for the core feature, so I will not go into detail here. People can explore them on their own.

----------

Still feels like something is missing?

Q: What if I simply cannot get through the text?
A: Then you can turn the text into audio and listen while reading.

Q: What if there is a part I do not understand?
A: Then you can directly select the confusing text and ask AI any question you want.

Q: What if there is a word I do not know, and your system did not mark it?
A: That is actually one of my favorite features. It is called instant highlight translation. Just select the unknown word and the translation appears automatically with no extra clicking, and it is context-aware too. It is much better than the built-in dictionary feature on macOS.

## Web Version

![Xiaohongshu image](/images/apps/read-easy/red-note.jpeg)

Now you can directly read nearly 50 handpicked English sources on the Read Easy website itself, and AI summaries are supported as well, which helps you judge whether an article is worth your time before you commit to reading it.

This new version makes it easier to access and read English information, and it also lets you learn English by reading things you are actually interested in.

The earlier form was a browser extension. The idea was to use AI-generated annotations to lower the difficulty of reading in English. But the extension still was not quite "easy" enough. Many people do not already have the habit of reading English articles. Some do not know what is worth reading. And extensions are not always well suited to the content structure of every site. That is what led to the new version.

Although the technology underneath is old, RSS, I prefer to think of it as a lightweight browser.

The information source opening in the sidebar feels a bit like opening websites in Arc's sidebar, except that the content form itself has changed.

RSS often makes people think of knowledge management: unread items, read items, read-later lists, and a heavy sense of obligation. A website feels lighter. You can open it casually and close it casually.

Yes, RSS does provide useful extras. It can tell you when a site has updated, which makes it easier not to miss an article.

But I do not think any book is absolutely required reading, or any article absolutely must not be missed, especially in an era where needed information can usually be found quickly.

I call it a lightweight browser because the current functionality is still limited. For example, it cannot yet open arbitrary websites, it is mainly focused on English information sources, and other parts of the product are also designed around the goal of making English reading easier.

In short, it is like a lightweight browser dedicated to exploring the English internet.

But none of those labels are the important part.

What matters is not the form itself or how we define it. What matters is whether it is useful to you.

If you want to get information beyond the Chinese internet, or if you want to learn English, then perhaps it can be a little useful.
