This has been the main thing on my mind while building my personal site recently. I do not want to list projects. I want to place them inside an environment that can be felt.

> When people remember a sense of space instead of a button, the product starts to feel warm.

## From information structure to spatial structure

Traditional portfolios usually arrange everything by time or category. That is clear enough, but it can also become a very efficient archive with very little feeling.

I want to try something else: turn essays, experiments, product pages, and links into rooms, windows, or objects. The user is not just reading a directory. They are entering a scene.

## Why this works well for a personal site

A personal site is not a document system. It should naturally carry the owner's taste. If you like operating-system interfaces, window-based reading, or organizing work as a set of openable apps, those choices are not decoration alone. They explain how you think about products.

That kind of expression also makes it easier to place writing, experiments, product notes, and external links inside one coherent narrative.

## The boundary I want to keep

Form should never overpower usability. No matter how playful the scene becomes, a direct path to the content still has to exist.

Exploration should feel like a reward, not a requirement. That has been the principle behind these recent iterations.
